| | |

> **One-sentence answer**
> The **test-out** is the **widest** (many topics, each tested as a skill); **AP Precalculus** is **narrower but deeper** on functions and modeling; **AP Calculus BC** is the **biggest and deepest**, and it expects **all** of precalculus to be automatic.

## 2. The Big Picture

### How the three relate

```text
┌─ TROY HONORS TEST-OUT (~108 items) ───────┐
│ RCS exam for credit ≈ inside here         │
│                                           │
│  ┌─ Troy test-out (~102) ───┐             │
│  │ = Honors − derivatives   │             │
│  └──────────────────────────┘             │
│                                           │
│                        ┌─ AP PRECALCULUS (Units 1-3) ────────┐
│ TEST-OUT ONLY          │ BOTH             │ AP ONLY          │
│ conics, 3D, matrices,  │ function core:   │ rates of change, │
│ triangle trig,         │ poly, rational,  │ data modeling,   │
│ DeMoivre, probability, │ exp/log, trig,   │ model choice,    │
│ limits, derivatives    │ polar            │ semi-log plots   │
└────────────────────────┼──────────────────┘                  │
                         └─────────────────────────────────────┘
                                ║  foundation
                                ▼
            ┌─ AP CALCULUS BC ───────────────────────┐
            │ all of Calculus AB (limits,            │
            │ derivatives, integrals, diff. eq.)     │
            │ + parametric / polar / vector calc     │
            │ + infinite series, Taylor series       │
            └────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Containment, in symbols:**

$$\text{Troy} \;\subset\; \text{Troy Honors}, \qquad \text{RCS} \;\approx\!\subset\; \text{Troy Honors}, \qquad \text{Test-out} \;\cap\; \text{AP Precalc} \;=\; \text{function core}$$

$$\text{Test-out} \;\not\subset\; \text{AP Precalc}, \qquad \text{AP Precalc} \;\not\subset\; \text{Test-out}, \qquad \text{BC} \;\text{uses}\; (\text{Test-out} \cup \text{AP Precalc}) \;\text{as its foundation}$$

### What sits in each region

| Test-out only | Both (function core) | AP Precalculus only | AP Calculus BC |
|---|---|---|---|
| conics, rotation of conics | functions & transformations | rates of change, "change in tandem" | **All of Calculus AB:** limits & continuity |
| 3D space, cross product, lines & planes | composition, inverse functions | modeling from data (regression) | derivatives & applications |
| matrices, Gaussian elimination, linear programming | polynomial & rational functions | choosing & validating a model | integrals & applications |
| Law of Sines / Cosines *(AP Unit 4, not on exam)* | exponential & logarithmic functions | semi-log plots | differential equations |
| DeMoivre, powers & roots of complex numbers | trig graphs, identities, equations, inverse trig | graphing-calculator work | **BC only:** parametric, polar, vector calculus |
| partial fractions, Rational Zero Test | polar coordinates & graphs | | infinite series, Taylor series |
| series, induction, counting, probability | arithmetic & geometric sequences, binomial theorem | | integration by parts, partial fractions, Euler's method, logistic growth |
| limits, derivatives *(Honors / RCS)* | | | |

- The **test-out** is the largest precalculus set. It includes whole chapters that AP Precalculus does not test: conics, 3D space and vectors, matrices, triangle trig, induction and probability, limits, and (Honors) derivatives.
- **AP Precalculus** shares the **function core** with the test-out, but it adds things the test-out never asks: explaining **rates of change**, building and **choosing models from data**, and **semi-log plots**. It also uses a graphing calculator.
- **AP Calculus BC** is not "more precalc." It is a new subject built **on top of** both sets. Every precalculus skill becomes a tool you must use without thinking.

## 4. Who Has More? Who Is Broader?

**Counting topics (precalculus exams):**

$$\underbrace{\text{Troy Honors}}_{\approx 108\ \text{items}} \;\supset\; \underbrace{\text{Troy}}_{\approx 102\ \text{items}} \quad\text{and}\quad \text{RCS} \;\approx\!\subset\; \text{Troy Honors} \qquad\qquad \text{AP Precalculus: } 44 \text{ tested topics}$$

- **Troy regular ⊂ Troy Honors.** The two lists are word-for-word the same, except that Honors adds a **Derivatives** section.
- **RCS ≈ ⊂ Troy Honors.** RCS is a short list. Almost everything on it is also on Troy Honors. It names three things explicitly: **nonlinear inequalities**, **independent events**, and the **SSA ambiguous case**.
- **Test-out vs. AP Precalculus: neither contains the other.**
  - Both have the **function core**: polynomial, rational, exponential, logarithmic, trigonometric, and polar functions.
  - Only the test-out has conics, 3D, matrices, triangle trig, DeMoivre, partial fractions, the Rational Zero Test, series, induction, probability, limits, and derivatives. (AP puts some of these in Unit 4, which is not on the exam.)
  - Only AP Precalculus has rates-of-change reasoning, data modeling, model validation, semi-log plots, and graphing-calculator work.

**Conclusion:**

| | Breadth (how many kinds of topics) | Depth (how hard each topic is pushed) |
|---|---|---|
| Precalculus test-out (Troy Honors) | ★★★★★ widest | ★★ skills and procedures |
| AP Precalculus | ★★★ function families only | ★★★★ reasoning, context, justification |
| AP Calculus BC | ★★★★ (a whole new subject) | ★★★★★ deepest; assumes all precalc is automatic |

## 5. How Deep Does Each Exam Go?

| | Precalculus test-out | AP Precalculus | AP Calculus BC |
|---|---|---|---|
| Typical verb | **find, solve, simplify, graph, evaluate** | **explain, interpret, justify, construct a model** | **justify using a theorem, analyze, set up and evaluate** |
| What is tested | correct procedure and exact answer | understanding of how a function behaves; connecting graph, table, formula, and words | combining many precalc skills inside one calculus argument |
| Calculator | scientific / none → **exact values** by hand | half with graphing calculator (regression, intersections) | mostly no calculator; graphing calculator for numerical work |
| Free response | show work, partial credit | 4 questions × 6 points, units and context required | 6 questions × 9 points, justification required |

### One topic, three depths: exponential functions

- **Test-out:** Solve $3\cdot 2^{x}=48$.  → $2^x=16$, so $x=4$. *(One correct procedure.)*
- **AP Precalculus:** A table shows a population every 2 years. *Explain* why an exponential model fits better than a linear one. *Construct* the model, and *interpret* the base in context. For example, "the population grows by 15% per year." *(Reasoning with data and context.)*
- **AP Calculus BC:** A population satisfies $\dfrac{dP}{dt}=0.3P\left(1-\dfrac{P}{500}\right)$. Find $\lim_{t\to\infty}P(t)$ and the population at which it grows fastest, and *justify*. *(Needs exponential functions, limits, derivatives, and algebra, all at once.)*

### One topic, three depths: trig identities

- **Test-out:** Verify $\dfrac{1-\cos 2x}{\sin 2x}=\tan x$, or use a product-to-sum formula. *(Full list of identities.)*
- **AP Precalculus:** Only Pythagorean, sum/difference, and double-angle identities for sine and cosine are tested. They are used to *rewrite* a function and to *explain* its graph.
- **AP Calculus BC:** You are expected to *choose* the right identity with no hint. For example, evaluate $\int \sin^2 x\,dx$ by rewriting $\sin^2 x=\dfrac{1-\cos 2x}{2}$.

## 6. What This Means for Our Course

1. **Breadth comes from the test-out.** The **Troy Honors list is our master list**, so every item on all three district syllabi is taught (see `00_Course_Overview.md` §4).
2. **Depth comes from AP Precalculus.** We borrow AP-style "explain / justify / interpret" questions (homework Part C) so that you understand functions, not just compute with them.
3. **Everything points to BC.** Each lesson has a **"Why this matters in AP Calculus BC"** box (see `00_Course_Overview.md` §5). Topics that are **BC-only** later, such as partial fractions, polar, parametric, vectors, and series, are ones we already meet here. That gives you a head start.
4. **This means a lot to remember.** A wide test-out list plus BC formulas means **many formulas and procedures**. Use the routine in `L00_Active_Recall.md`: recall after every block, recall before sleep, and always recall the **reason** behind each formula.

---

> **Summary**
> 1. **Troy regular ⊂ Troy Honors** (Honors adds derivatives). The **RCS** list sits almost entirely inside Troy Honors.
> 2. **Test-out vs. AP Precalculus:** they share the function core. The **test-out is broader** (conics, 3D, matrices, triangle trig, probability, limits). **AP Precalculus is deeper** (rates of change, modeling from data, justification).
> 3. **AP Calculus BC** = all of Calculus AB + parametric/polar/vector calculus + infinite series. It is the **largest and deepest**, and every precalc skill must be automatic.
> 4. Test-out questions ask you to **do** a procedure. AP Precalculus asks you to **explain** it. BC asks you to **use many procedures together** and **justify** the result.
